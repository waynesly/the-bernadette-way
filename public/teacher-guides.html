SHARED PRACTICE

Set every experience up well.

Prepare

Use a teacher device to create the roster and print individual code cards. Test one student code before the session.

Frame it

Say: “This is a snapshot of today. It helps us notice what supports learning and how we can practise together.”

Follow up

Use results to plan routines, group talk and individual check-ins. Never publish, rank or compare results.

Safeguarding and privacy

  • Keep teacher passwords, class access codes and printed student codes private.
  • Project class-level patterns without names; discuss individual profiles only with the student and relevant staff.
  • Use professional judgement alongside the data. A self-report is one source of information, not a diagnosis.
  • For a student who is distressed, pause the activity and follow your school’s usual wellbeing and safeguarding processes.
  • At the end of a cycle, use the teacher reset controls to clear data in line with school retention expectations.

YEARS 3–6 · 12–15 MINUTES

My Gifts, My Growth

This experience supports a whole-class conversation about belonging, collaboration, learning, voice, self-regulation and contribution. It uses short, accessible statements and provides a class picture for planning.

Set-up checklist

  1. Open Teacher set-up and select My Gifts, My Growth.
  2. Create a class, entering one student name per line. Keep the class ID and access code together in a secure staff location.
  3. Print private student join cards. Give each child only their own card.
  4. Open the appropriate window: Move Up Day, or the beginning or mid-term check-in for Terms 1–4. Historical windows remain available for existing records.
  5. Ask students to visit the My Gifts, My Growth page and enter their private code.

Move Up Day · differentiated Years 3–6 lessons

Use the dedicated Move Up Day teaching sequence for four age-appropriate transition lessons: Year 3 belonging and welcome; Year 4 teamwork and listening; Year 5 humble leadership and service; Year 6 responsible leadership and planning for others. Each includes a Catholic connection to St Bernadette, Discover → Grow → Shine activities, timing, teacher prompts, inclusion guidance and Term 1 follow-up. Open or print the full lesson sequence.

Facilitation script

Try saying: “We are going to notice habits that help our class learn and belong. Answer for yourself today. Your teacher will look for class patterns so we can choose useful things to practise together.”

Access

Offer headphones, a quiet spot or adult read-aloud support where usual adjustments call for them.

During the check-in

Encourage first thoughts. Avoid explaining what a “good” answer looks like.

When finished

Invite students to read their private next step; they may choose whether to share it.

Three-stage follow-up

After the check-in, invite each student to identify a strength (Discover), practise one small habit (Grow), and notice a helpful action for others (Shine). Use the student summary prompt; no extra score or public sharing is required.

Three low-prep follow-ups

  • Belonging: co-create a “how we include people” routine for group work.
  • Collaboration: practise building on another person’s idea before adding your own.
  • Self-regulation: agree on a short pause-and-reset routine for tricky moments.
Timing across the year: begin with Move Up Day to introduce strengths and belonging. Revisit learning at the beginning and midpoint of each term, using the nine check-in windows to notice growth, practise classroom habits and celebrate contributions over time.

YEAR 5 · 96 QUESTIONS · 30–40 MINUTES

Lead with Heart

This individual strengths discovery invites Year 5 students to notice 24 character strengths and make a connection between their strengths, leadership and service to others.

Before students begin

  1. Open the Year 5 roster & codes page and create the class list.
  2. Print or securely distribute the private join cards. Plan one uninterrupted session; students can pause and resume if needed.
  3. Tell students the 96 self-ratings take longer than the Classroom Check-in and that honest, thoughtful answers are more useful than “perfect” ones.
  4. Prepare a quiet extension for early finishers, such as writing one example of a strength they used this week.

Facilitation script

Try saying: “This adventure helps you notice strengths you already use. It is not a scorecard or a label. Your results may change as you grow, and every strength can be practised.”

After students receive results

Notice

Ask students to choose one result that feels familiar and find a real example from school or home.

Connect

Link one signature strength to a leadership action: helping, organising, encouraging, persevering or speaking up.

Practise

Set one small challenge for the week and return to it in a brief reflection.

Three-stage follow-up

Ask students to name a strength they recognise (Discover), select a leadership challenge (Grow), then describe an actual act of service and its effect in their private journal (Shine). Revisit the next goal without ranking students or requiring public disclosure.

Four follow-up learning ideas

  • Strengths spotting: during a collaborative task, students name a strength they noticed in an action—not in a person’s identity.
  • Leadership role cards: match class responsibilities to behaviours such as fairness, teamwork, curiosity and perseverance.
  • Service design: ask groups to identify a school need and propose one strengths-based contribution.
  • Journal revisit: after one week, ask: “What did you try? What difference did it make? What is your next small goal?”
Important: signature strengths are conversation starters. Do not use them to allocate permanent roles, limit opportunities or make judgments about capability.

Reflect, adapt and follow through.

After a Move Up Day lesson, classroom check-in or Year 5 leadership activity, use the printable staff implementation and reflection record. Capture the learning intention, Discover → Grow → Shine observations, Catholic identity, inclusive adjustments, anonymous evidence, one next teaching action and a review point. The form does not submit or store student information; print or save it only to approved school storage.

Protect student privacy: do not include student names, join codes, individual scores, identifiable journal entries or sensitive disclosures in this record. Use the school's existing safeguarding process for individual concerns. Open the reflection record →

IMPLEMENTATION EVIDENCE · STAFF REVIEW

Summarise evidence without identifying students.

Use the anonymous implementation evidence summary to distinguish verified product testing, actual classroom delivery and educational outcomes. The template includes reporting scope, aggregate participation (only where verified and approved), Discover → Grow → Shine observations, Catholic identity, limitations and next evaluation steps. Leave unknown figures blank; do not count test data as classroom participation or claim impact without evidence.

Privacy first: never include names, student codes, individual scores, private reflections or small groups that could identify students. Obtain school approval before sharing evidence externally. Open the evidence summary →
