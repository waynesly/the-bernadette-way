Move Up Day: Discover · Grow · Shine

Four differentiated transition lessons for the class students are moving into. Help every child feel welcomed, recognise their God-given gifts, practise a positive habit and use that gift to help someone else.

Back to teaching guides
Before the day (teacher preparation). Select the Move Up Day window in the My Gifts, My Growth teacher dashboard, check the class roster and distribute individual join codes privately. Allow about 12–15 minutes for the optional student check-in, in addition to the 35–45-minute lesson below. Use paper alternatives or usual adjustments where needed. Move Up Day uses the existing beginning-of-year question set; this teaching sequence does not change scoring or student records.
Shared Catholic foundation. St Bernadette modelled humility, courage, faith and care for others. Explain that each person has God-given dignity and gifts; our gifts are for belonging, learning and service, not for comparing or ranking children. Invite a short prayer or quiet reflection appropriate to the class; students can participate respectfully without having to disclose beliefs or personal experiences.

Year 3 · Transition into Year 3

I belong, and I have gifts

35–40 minutes + optional check-in

Learning intention: Notice a strength in everyday actions and practise helping a new classmate feel welcome. Materials: paper gift shapes, pencils, a class belonging display.

Teacher opening: “Like St Bernadette, we do not have to be the loudest person to make a difference. Each of us matters, and small acts of kindness help us belong.”

1 · Discover (10 min)

Model a simple action (“I helped someone find a place to sit”). Students draw or write one thing they enjoy doing or a helpful action they have tried. Invite optional pair-sharing; no one has to name a ‘best’ strength.

2 · Grow (10 min)

Role-play a new student arriving, joining a game or needing help. Practise one friendly sentence: “Would you like to join us?” or “Can I show you where to go?” Let students rehearse with a partner or adult.

3 · Shine (10 min)

Each student chooses one small welcome action for Move Up Day. Add anonymous or first-name-only gift shapes to a class display with permission. Ask: “How might that action help someone feel safe and included?”

Close (5 min): Quietly reflect: “Thank you for the gifts we bring. Help us notice and welcome others.” Evidence: teacher observes welcoming language and participation, not scores. Revisit: use the agreed welcome routine in the first week of Term 1.

Year 4 · Transition into Year 4

Our gifts grow together

40 minutes + optional check-in

Learning intention: Recognise different strengths in a team and practise listening, fairness and cooperation. Materials: paper, markers and a simple group-building task.

Teacher opening: “St Bernadette listened and showed courage. We show respect for everyone's dignity when we make room for different ideas.”

1 · Discover (10 min)

Students recall a time they helped a group learn, play or solve a problem. Collect examples of actions—listening, encouraging, organising, persevering—without assigning labels to classmates.

2 · Grow (15 min)

In small groups, design a welcoming classroom poster or solve a low-stakes building challenge. Practise two routines: each person gets a turn, and each speaker's idea is acknowledged before the next is added.

3 · Shine (10 min)

Groups decide one way their teamwork can benefit another class member (for example, creating a shared help sign or invitation). Students complete “I helped our team by…” privately or voluntarily aloud.

Close (5 min): Ask, “Whose idea helped our group think differently?” Do not single out students who prefer not to speak. Evidence: note turn-taking and inclusive actions. Revisit: agree a listening norm for Term 1 group work.

Year 5 · Transition into Year 5

My gifts can serve others

40–45 minutes + optional check-in

Learning intention: Connect strengths to practical responsibility and humble leadership. Materials: scenario cards, sticky notes, a class service ideas board.

Teacher opening: “St Bernadette's courage and humility remind us that leadership is not about status. We can lead with heart when we notice what someone needs and respond with care.”

1 · Discover (10 min)

Invite students to describe a time they used courage, kindness, fairness or perseverance. Discuss how different gifts can contribute to the same goal. These are examples, not fixed identities.

2 · Grow (15 min)

Groups select a school scenario: someone is left out, a shared space needs care, or a team is struggling. Choose a small leadership response, assign fair roles and rehearse how to invite others rather than take over.

3 · Shine (10 min)

Each student writes a realistic service action to try in the coming week and how they will notice whether it helped. Sharing is optional. Discuss why dignity and consent matter when helping someone.

Close (5 min): Ask, “How can we lead quietly and kindly?” Evidence: record chosen actions and revisit outcomes, not rankings. Revisit: link the activity to the separate Lead with Heart Year 5 strengths and leadership pathway when appropriate; do not require its 96-question assessment on Move Up Day.

Year 6 · Transition into Year 6

Shine through responsible leadership

40–45 minutes + optional check-in

Learning intention: Plan an inclusive, achievable act of service that builds belonging and supports younger students. Materials: school-need scenarios, planning sheet, markers.

Teacher opening: “St Bernadette used courage and humility to live her faith. In Year 6, leadership means noticing others, listening well and using our gifts responsibly.”

1 · Discover (10 min)

Students reflect privately on strengths they have practised and what helps them collaborate. Identify a real need within school life—welcome, playground inclusion, care for shared spaces—without naming or diagnosing classmates.

2 · Grow (15 min)

Teams plan one small service action with a clear purpose, roles, adult approval and an accessibility check. Practise listening to the people being helped, sharing responsibility and changing plans when feedback suggests it.

3 · Shine (10 min)

Students prepare a short service commitment: “We will… so that… We will know it helped when…” Invite optional sharing, and agree when the teacher will review the action after transition.

Close (5 min): Ask, “How will we know our leadership made belonging easier for someone else?” Evidence: a feasible, respectful service plan, not a competitive score. Revisit: check progress in Term 1 and adjust the plan with student feedback.

Teacher facilitation and follow-through

  1. Before: Confirm students are entering the year level they are moving into; prepare differentiated materials, access adjustments and a private option for reflection. Explain that the check-in is a snapshot, not a test or a label.
  2. During: Teach the year-level lesson. If using the digital check-in, open Move Up Day in the teacher dashboard, distribute codes privately and allow students to complete independently with appropriate support. Never coach answers.
  3. After: Look at anonymous class patterns and student-chosen actions. Plan one inclusive routine for the first weeks of Term 1; keep individual results confidential.
  4. Follow-up: Revisit the chosen action at the beginning of Term 1, then use the beginning and mid-term windows across the year to notice changes. Do not assume changes in scores alone demonstrate impact.

Inclusion and safeguarding

Offer drawing, verbal rehearsal, scribing, alternative communication, extra processing time or a quiet workspace as needed. Students may opt out of public sharing. Avoid asking children to reveal personal distress, family circumstances or another student's needs. Escalate concerns through existing school safeguarding processes. Do not publicly display individual assessment scores or private journal entries.

Reflection questions for staff

  • Who was able to participate, and what adjustments improved access?
  • What did students do that showed welcome, collaboration or service?
  • What classroom routine will we deliberately practise in Term 1?
  • What should we revisit at the next check-in without treating scores as labels?
