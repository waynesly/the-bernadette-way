Who was St Bernadette?

Bernadette Soubirous was born in Lourdes, France, in 1844. Her family experienced poverty and she faced illness and difficulties with schooling. In 1858, aged fourteen, she reported apparitions of a lady at the grotto of Massabielle. Catholics recognise these as apparitions of Our Lady of Lourdes. Bernadette faced questioning and disbelief, yet remained steadfast in recounting what she experienced. Later she entered religious life in Nevers, where she lived a life of prayer and service.

Her story reminds us that dignity does not depend on popularity, achievement or power. Humility, honesty, courage and care can make a lasting difference.

Why this matters in a Catholic school

We believe every person is made in the image and likeness of God (Genesis 1:27) and has inherent dignity. Our gifts are not labels or rankings. They are opportunities for growth, stewardship, friendship, leadership and service. Jesus teaches us to use what we have been given for the good of others (1 Peter 4:10; Mark 10:45).

At St Bernadette's, we discover who we are, grow into who God calls us to be, and shine by sharing our gifts with others.

Think, talk and act

For students

What gift can I recognise in myself? When have I shown courage or humility? Who could benefit from my help this week?

For teachers

Introduce Bernadette's story before strengths activities. Invite children to recognise gifts without comparison, practise a strength and reflect on whom their actions helped. Connect reflection with prayer, Scripture and practical acts of service.

For families

Ask your child what they discovered about themselves, which gift they want to grow and how they can share it at home or in the community.
